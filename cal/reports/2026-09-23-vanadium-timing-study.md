## The design confound (read first)

The one-click sequence walked the same 12 raster sites in a fixed order, one lap per DELAY row.  Verified from the ledger: lap = position//12, and each lap contains exactly one delay:

| lap | delay | prior shots/site | period→sites |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | 1 | 0 (fresh) | each period always on the same 3 of the 12 sites |
| 2 | 5 | ~7–10 | (same mapping every lap) |
| 3 | 10 | ~14–20 | |
| 4 | 20 | ~21–30 | |
| 5 | 50 | ~28–40 | |

**Consequences.** (i) The *delay* main effect is identical to *crater depth* — delay 1 is fresh, delay 50 has ~30–40 prior shots per site — so an intensity change with delay cannot be attributed to gate timing alone. (ii) The *period* main effect is period **plus** a fixed site-set offset (each period always on the same 3 sites), but that offset is common to every delay row, so the period ladder *within a delay* and the delay×period interaction are clean if site effects are additive. (iii) Background-robust, intensity-independent quantities (peak-HEIGHT line-pair ratios with close side bands, FWHM) carry the physics verdicts; NET-AREA quantities are NOT reliable here because a period-scaled detector fixed pattern (§3) sits under the lines and biases areas.  run1 used a different lap/site order and a shallower depth range (0–20 prior shots), which lets an explicit regression separate the DELAY (plasma-timing) effect from crater DEPTH (§2.2).

## 2. Q1 — Is SHORT DELAY better?  Delay IS a real timing effect

In run2 delay ≡ crater depth, so run2 alone cannot say whether a delay trend is timing or depth.  §2.1 shows the run2 trends (period 1, background-robust peak-height ratios); §2.2 uses run1 — shallower (0–20 prior shots/site) and a different lap/site order — to REGRESS the delay effect against depth explicitly.

V I is not significant on either axis over this shallow range.

**Q1 answer:** short delay genuinely captures the hotter, more-ionised early plasma (higher V II and V II/V I).  It is a real lever on ionic-vs-neutral content, to be chosen on FRESH sites; it is not merely the fresh-crater advantage.  Hα stays at noise (SNR≈3) at all delays even at P=50, so early air contamination is not a short-delay problem here.

## 3. Q2 — Is SHORT PERIOD better?  Period is a detector exposure, not a plasma gate

Owner's question: *does integrated line signal scale with period — is 'period' really the gate width?*  Two facts settle it: (1) V I line signal is FLAT with period; (2) the period-scaled *extra* signal is an instrument dark / fixed pattern, not plasma continuum.

### 3.1 Line signal is flat with period

Slope of Σ V I line area vs period per delay (counts/unit) with the fractional change P1→P50 — indistinguishable from zero everywhere:

| delay | Σ V I line slope (±SE) | Δ P1→P50 |
|--|--|--|
| 1 | -0.57±2.00 | 1% |
| 5 | 0.65±1.44 | 1% |
| 10 | 1.29±1.94 | 3% |
| 20 | 1.17±1.08 | 2% |
| 50 | 0.53±1.51 | -0% |

### 3.2 The period-scaled 'continuum' is a detector fixed pattern

If the period-dependent background were plasma continuum it would scale with the plasma brightness, i.e. differ between delays.  It does not.  The P50−P1 DIFFERENCE spectrum (400–900 nm) is nearly IDENTICAL at every delay, even though the plasma line signal differs ~2× across delays:

| difference spectrum | d=1 | d=5 | d=10 | d=20 | d=50 |
|--|--|--|--|--|--|
| corr vs deepest (d=50) | 0.97 | 0.98 | 0.98 | 0.98 | 1.00 |

Far-NIR (880–900 nm) 10th-percentile level (counts) vs period — it moves with PERIOD identically at every delay and goes NEGATIVE, the signature of a period-scaled dark/fixed pattern with imperfect vendor subtraction (a real plasma continuum cannot be negative):

|--|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | 46 | 15 | -63 | -212 |
| 5 | 39 | 4 | -82 | -229 |
| 10 | 28 | -6 | -96 | -248 |
| 20 | 26 | -10 | -105 | -263 |
| 50 | 21 | -9 | -117 | -266 |

| delay\period | 10 | 25 | 50 |  (run1, same pattern)
|--|--|--|--|
| 5 | 19 | -66 | -202 |
| 10 | 13 | -69 | -215 |
| 20 | -8 | -81 | -233 |

**Reading.**  The difference-spectrum correlations are ~0.9+ across delays and the NIR level goes strongly negative with period in the same steps at every delay (and in run1), so the period-scaled signal is a detector exposure/fixed-pattern term, NOT plasma emission.  **Period behaves like a detector exposure that is longer than the plasma lifetime: it adds no line photons, only fixed pattern + noise, collapsing SNR and score.**  (Note d50/P1 still carries ~40–50 % of the d1 line height, so the line emission is NOT 'complete within one period unit' — the earlier draft's gate-width reading is withdrawn.)  Verdict: **short period is unambiguously better; use the minimum period, and the score/continuum gain is instrumental, so quantitative work needs a per-period dark/fixed-pattern measurement (laser off/blocked) subtracted.**

## 4. Q3 — Does a long gate MIMIC self-absorption (temperature mixing)?

**The owner hypothesis — that a shorter integration reduces APPARENT optical thickness by excluding changing-T plasma — cannot be tested with the PERIOD knob:** period is a detector exposure longer than the plasma (§3), so a longer period adds no new plasma-time to the gate, only fixed pattern.  The genuine opacity probes below are therefore read against PERIOD (should be flat if opacity is period-independent) and against DELAY (the only real lever on the temporal mix).

### 4.1 (a) T-independent pair 417.94/415.97 — background-robust PEAK-HEIGHT ratio

Peak height (local max minus close side bands) is immune to the period-scaled fixed pattern that corrupts net areas.  This branching pair shares an upper level, so its ratio is T-independent; a move is real opacity.

Thin height ratio R₀≈2.05.  **The height ratio is flat with period** at each delay (d1 ≈ 0.75 across P1–P50; SD ≈ 0.02), confirming the earlier draft's τ-falling-with-period was a NET-AREA artefact of the period-scaled background, not an opacity change.

The ratio sits well below R₀≈2.05 — even below 1 — at every period, i.e. the strong 417.94 member is measured no brighter than the weak 415.97, so it is heavily self-absorbed and/or 415.97 is blended.  A quantitative τ is therefore NOT quoted (the escape-factor inversion rails for a below-1 ratio); the defensible statements are (i) real, period-INDEPENDENT compression, and (ii) a FALL with delay/lap (0.75 at d1 → 0.56 at d50).

**OPEN:** whether that delay fall is more opacity in the later/deeper plasma or a growing blend on the weak 415.97 member is not separated (delay≡depth in run2; the absolute ratio is also sensitive to weak-member peak-finding in this dense forest — the robust facts are flatness with period and the fall with delay).

## 7. Verdicts

**Q1 (short delay).** Short delay is a REAL plasma-timing lever, not just the fresh-crater advantage.  run2 delay ≡ depth, but run1 (shallower, different lap order) separates them (§2.2): V II 311 falls with DELAY at -0.0202±0.0045/unit (4.5σ) with depth NOT significant (-0.0023±0.0044); V I 411 is not significant on either axis.  So the ionic (V II) content and V II/V I ratio genuinely drop as the plasma cools with delay.  Choose delay for the ionic-vs-neutral balance you want, on FRESH sites (short delay = hotter, more V II).  FWHM is flat (no ne/Stark trend); Hα stays at noise at all delays (no early-air issue).
**Q2 (short period).** Period-mean score: P1=0.504, P10=0.484, P25=0.461, P50=0.415.  Score falls monotonically with period at every delay, reproducing run1.  Mechanism (§3): V I line signal is FLAT with period (Δ within a few % , slope≈0), and the period-scaled 'extra' signal is a detector dark/fixed pattern, NOT plasma continuum — its P50−P1 difference spectrum is ~identical across delays (corr ~0.9+) though line signal differs 2×, and the far-NIR level goes negative with period identically at every delay and in run1.  Period is a detector exposure longer than the plasma: it captures no more line light, only fixed pattern + noise, so SNR and score collapse.  **Use the minimum period.**  Best cell 5/1 (score 0.539), but that score gain is partly instrumental — calibration needs a per-period dark/fixed-pattern subtraction and a FIXED period.
**Q3 (does a long gate mimic self-absorption via T-mixing?).** The owner hypothesis CANNOT be tested with the period knob: period is a detector exposure beyond the plasma lifetime (§3), so a longer period does not add changing-T plasma to the gate.  On the background-robust peak-HEIGHT ratios (§4.1) the T-independent pair 417/415 is compressed below R₀ at EVERY period and FLAT with period (d1 ≈ 0.75 across P1–P50) — genuine self-absorption that is period-INDEPENDENT.  The earlier draft's τ falling with period, and the 478/475 'T-mixing' signal, were NET-AREA/fixed-pattern artefacts (475.39 gains an additive offset with period at every delay; §4.2) and are withdrawn.  **Verdict: the hypothesis is NOT supported and not testable here; genuine SA on the strong low-E_lower V I multiplets is real and period-independent, so an SA correction is required at any period.  Only DELAY changes the temporal mix (V II/V I).**  OPEN: the 417/415 height ratio falls with delay/lap (0.75 at d1 → 0.56 at d50) — more opacity in the later/deeper plasma, or a growing blend on the weak member; not separated (delay≡depth).
**Q4 (frame drops).** 23/59 batches stored <10 shots.  Drops are mild (never worse than 7/10), cluster at the deeper laps (d10–d50) rather than tracking period, and moving them changes nothing; consistent with occasional retrieval loss, not a timing effect.

## 8. Recommended settings & next study design

- **Acquisition for the calibration campaign:** use the **minimum period** (longer period only adds detector fixed pattern + noise).  Choose **delay for the ionic-vs-neutral balance** you want — short delay (~1–5 units) gives the hotter, more V II plasma — on FRESH sites.  Best measured cell 5/1 (score 0.539), but note the score's period dependence is largely instrumental.
- **Fixed-pattern / dark:** because the period-scaled background is a detector fixed pattern with imperfect vendor subtraction, calibration studies must (i) hold the period FIXED, and (ii) acquire a per-setting dark/fixed-pattern reference (laser off or beam blocked) and subtract it before any area/continuum quantity is used.  Use background-robust peak HEIGHTS with close side bands, not net areas, until this is done.
- **Self-absorbed resonance V I lines** (437–439, 410–411, 478/475): SA is real and period-independent (417/415 compressed at every period).  Keep an SA correction from the multiplet-ratio τ (measured on peak heights) for quantitative work and prefer higher-E_lower V I lines for the Boltzmann thermometer.
- **Next study design (break the confounds):** randomise/interleave the (delay,period) order ACROSS laps so delay is no longer one-lap-one-delay; put every condition on a fresh site (or record shots-per-site and regress it out, as in §2.2); keep ≥3 replicates.  Add a pure depth series (fixed timing, many laps on one site) and a laser-off dark/period series to measure the crater-depth response and the fixed pattern directly.  Extend delay down to 0–2 on fresh sites to map the early plasma.

## 9. Unverified / limits

- **Period-scaled detector fixed pattern.** The period-dependent background is an instrument dark/fixed pattern with imperfect vendor subtraction (§3), so NET AREAS and any 'continuum' quantity are unreliable and are NOT used for physics; all opacity/ionisation results use background-robust peak HEIGHTS with close side bands.  A per-period laser-off dark reference is needed to remove it properly.
- **Delay ≡ crater depth in run2.** run2 alone cannot separate delay from depth; the delay-vs-depth verdict rests on the run1 regression (shallower range, independent lap/site order), whose depth range is only 0–20 prior shots — the separation is not guaranteed at greater depth.
- **Period ≡ period + fixed site set.** The period main effect carries a constant 3-site offset; the period ladder within a delay and the interaction are clean only under additive site effects (untested here).
- **Vendor timing units unverified.** All delay/period values are raw vendor units (ns/100 ns/µs unknown); nothing here fixes the unit absolutely.
- **Segment response uncorrected.** V II is mostly UV and V I mostly VIS, so the absolute V II/V I ratio is response-biased; only its relative TREND across conditions (response constant) is used.
- **ne / Stark not converted, T not quantitative.** VIS FWHM is instrument-limited with no clear delay trend, so no ne is derived; the V I Boltzmann slope-T is ill-constrained and contradictory (rises with delay while V II/V I falls), reported only for completeness.
- **τ from a homogeneous-slab escape factor** on peak-height ratios — a slab-average lower bound on peak optical depth; real gradients make it conservative.
- **417/415 falling with delay is OPEN** — late/deep opacity vs a growing weak-member blend is not separated (delay≡depth in run2).
- **V registration scatter (~1 px).** Heights use a ±0.18 nm local-max recentre; the 437–440 triplet is displaced/blended and not used quantitatively.
- Shift model rests on 1 Ar NIR anchor(s) on the fresh pool; global scatter ~50 pm.
